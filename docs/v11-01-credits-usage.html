Credits & Usage

The money screen. What every action costs, what's left, who spent it, and how to stop it running away. Everything below is read from the prototype, including what it forgot.

Why this screen exists

v11 asks each question 3 to 10 times instead of once. That is the single most valuable change in the release — and the most expensive. Without a meter, costs run away quietly and we find out at the end of the month.

So this screen does two jobs. The obvious one is control: show the balance, show the burn rate, stop the spending before it hurts. The second one is selling, and it is the more important of the two.

The business idea underneath

Customers connect their own AI keys, so the AI calls cost us nothing — and we charge nothing for them. Credits only meter what genuinely costs us money: crawls, scrapes, report generation and content drafts. Competitors charge per run because they pay per run. This screen is where a customer sees that difference. The prototype puts it in writing: "Rivals charge you per run because they pay per run."

07

Guardrails

Three settings that prevent the bad ending. This is the part that stops a runaway schedule draining a month's credits overnight.

Alert me at 30% / 20% / 10% remaining
Auto-pause schedules at 10% / 5% / Off
This project's cap No cap / 150 per month / Custom

Undefined What happens to a run already in flight when auto-pause triggers? Finish it, or kill it mid-way and refund? Must be decided before build.

08

Ledger

The line-by-line history. Five columns: When · Description · Reason · Delta · Balance. Exports to CSV.

Not built The table is empty in the prototype. The markup is <tbody id="ldg"></tbody> and no code ever fills it. The screen's own subtitle promises "Every debit, with what caused it" — and this is the part that would deliver it. It is the most important block on the page and the only one with no design at all.

Biggest gap on this screen

The Ledger needs designing before build. Decide now: does one group run write one row ("Group run · Savings · 25") or one row per engine call? That choice changes the database, the export, and whether a customer can ever reconcile a charge they dispute.

Sanity check on the allowance

500 credits a month = 125 a week, which matches what Schedules shows. But the demo customer's schedules forecast 525 a week — 4.2× over. That is deliberate: it shows the warning working. Worth confirming the real plan sizes are set against realistic run volumes, or every customer lands in a warning state on day one.

The rules the backend has to enforce

Drawn from behaviour visible across the prototype.

Check firstEstimate the cost before the action runs and show it. The Schedule dialog does this live: "At daily cadence this spends 175 credits a week. Your weekly allowance is 125."
BlockIf the balance is short, refuse and explain. The prototype has a dedicated dialog: "This run costs 1,000 credits. You have 308 remaining, so you're short by 692." Two ways out — Adjust run or Upgrade Plan. Never fail silently.
ChargeDebit when the action starts, and write a ledger row naming what caused it.
RefundCancelled work is returned — "Audit cancelled · 30 credits refunded". Also needs deciding: what happens on a failed run? Runs shows a 1.4% failure rate with automatic retries, and customers should not pay for our failures.
WarnFire an alert at the chosen threshold (30 / 20 / 10%).
StopAuto-pause schedules at 10% or 5%. Automations respect this too — one recipe carries the guardrail "never below 30% balance".
CapEnforce per-project limits independently of the workspace balance.
ResetRefill monthly. Undefined: does the reset fall on the calendar month or the signup anniversary, and do unused credits roll over? The screen says "resets in 18 days" without saying which.

What the backend needs

Nothing exotic. A balance, a price table, a ledger, and a check that runs before every billable action.

One decision that shapes everything

Credits are only worth metering if the numbers are real. Since LLM calls are free under BYOK, what we are actually charging for is our own compute — crawls, scrapes, report rendering, draft generation. Price those against what they genuinely cost to run, or the meter becomes arbitrary and customers will notice.

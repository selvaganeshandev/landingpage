Architecture

The shape of the product

Pixis Visibility is one of three products at Pixis (alongside Prism for paid campaigns and Adroom for creative). It is sold as a single SEO + GEO execution platform — the pitch is not "we measure AI visibility" but "we measure it and then publish the article that fixes it, in the same tool."

That framing is the whole competitive story. Every comparison page they publish against Profound, Peec, AthenaHQ, Semrush, Ahrefs, Conductor, Botify and Surfer lands on the same line: those tools stop at a dashboard, we go from gap → brief → draft → published page → measured impact. It is worth taking seriously because it is a defensible position, and because it is the axis on which they will be compared to us.

Ten modules make up the product. The two most interesting to us are the ones we do not have at all.

Read the source critically

Almost everything below is drawn from Pixis's own marketing and blog content. It describes what they claim to ship. Treat the methodology as the useful part — it is specific, internally consistent, and mostly correct on the underlying science — and treat the capability claims as unverified until someone runs a trial.

GEO audit

How they audit AI visibility

This is the strongest part of their thinking, and the part most worth copying. It rests on one honest admission that most of the category avoids: no AI engine publishes a visibility score, so every number is an outside estimate, and two tools can score the same brand 45 and 22 without the brand changing. Their answer is to make the methodology the product.

The prompt portfolio

Everything starts with a fixed, repeatable prompt set — not keywords. They define eight intent types, and a portfolio is considered weak if it does not cover all of them:

  • Branded — does this specific brand suit a given need
  • Category — best platforms in the space
  • Problem-led — a pain point described without naming a category
  • Use-case — which platforms support a specific workflow
  • Comparison — one brand weighed against another
  • Alternative — options beyond an incumbent
  • Validation — suitability for a context, e.g. regulated industries
  • Buying — which to choose for a specific requirement

They cite the IAB "Measuring Visibility in the AI Era" framework (Aug 2026), which classes any programme under 50 queries as exploratory rather than directional, and organises visibility into four layers: presence, prominence, portrayal, persuasion. Their own line is that a platform can measure the first three; persuasion needs downstream business data. That is a defensible, quotable position and we should hold the same one.

The measurement hierarchy

Five nested levels, and they argue most tools produce misleading numbers by collapsing them. This is genuinely the sharpest idea in their material:

The three-bucket diagnosis

Their free 15-minute audit — the one they use as the demo hook — maps every gap into one of three buckets, each with a different root cause. This is the piece that turns a dashboard into advice, and it is cheap for us to implement because we already hold all three signals:

  • Present and cited — healthy. Below 30% of prompts is materially weak.
  • Absent, competitors present — off-site authority problem. Fix is earned mentions on the third-party sources the engines actually cite, not another blog post. Urgent above 50%.
  • Nobody cited, generic answer — open lane. The model has no confident opinion yet; first comprehensive publisher takes the ground.

A fourth diagnosis sits alongside: present on some engines, absent on others — a retrieval problem, not a content problem, and each engine gets a different fix. ChatGPT leans on the Bing index (6–12 weeks to reflect changes); Perplexity runs live web search (2–4 weeks); Google AI Overviews draw ~97% of cited sources from Google's top 20, so it is an organic-ranking problem; Gemini favours structured data; Claude favours well-structured authoritative sources.

Methodology

Sampling: their 12 against our 1

This is the single most important finding in the research, and it is a genuine methodological gap rather than a missing feature.

AI answers are non-deterministic. Run the same prompt twice and the brands, sources and framing move. Semrush's own research found 40–60% of cited sources change month to month. Pixis's response is to run every prompt 12 times — 3 runs on each of 4 engines — and report variance-reduced aggregates. They then use it as the primary attack line against Peec, AthenaHQ, Conductor and AirOps, all of whom sample once (AirOps gates multi-session to Enterprise, and their own docs call single-session "just one snapshot").

Promptmaxx currently queries each prompt once per platform. In engine/core/prompt_analytics_processor.py the loop is one call per platform, and PromptAnalytics is keyed uniquely on (prompt, platform) — the schema itself has no room for a second run. Every headline number we show a customer rests on a single sample.

We already query six engines to their four. Three runs each puts us at 18 observations per prompt against their 12 — the strongest methodology claim in the category, from a change that is mostly a schema migration and a cost decision. It also unlocks a metric nobody is selling: a per-prompt stability score, telling a customer which of their wins are fragile.

SEO audit

The eight technical monitors

Their SEO side is deliberately unglamorous, and it is where our gap is widest — we have no technical audit capability at all. Nothing in the repo touches robots.txt, sitemaps, Core Web Vitals, broken links or schema validation. Our SEO module is rankings, backlinks, competitors, content gaps and share of voice; theirs adds a continuously-running site health layer underneath all of it.

Eight monitors run on a schedule and roll into a Technical Health score out of 100 with severity-ranked action items and threshold-based alerting — the team is notified when something changes, rather than having to go look.

One thing they get right that most competitors oversell

Their SEO audit guide is unusually honest about structured data: Google states plainly that schema is not required for its generative features and there is no special markup for them. Pixis repeats this rather than selling schema as a citation lever. If we ship a technical audit, we should hold the same line — it is a trust signal in a category full of magic-bullet claims.

Highest leverage

The AI crawler map

The single most useful, most concrete thing in their entire content library — and the cheapest for us to build. The insight is that AI crawlers do three different jobs, and only one of them affects whether you get cited. Teams routinely block the wrong ones.

The classic failure: a team blocks GPTBot and ClaudeBot to protect IP — a legitimate choice that does nothing to citations, because those are training bots. Meanwhile an over-broad CDN rule blocks OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot and quietly removes the brand from AI answers entirely. Most sites built or last audited before 2023 block AI crawlers by default.

Two caveats they publish, both worth carrying into our own copy because they are the kind of nuance that earns trust: Cloudflare documented in Aug 2025 that Perplexity uses undeclared crawlers rotating user-agents and IPs to evade directives — so robots.txt is advisory and real enforcement needs WAF rules. And Rutgers/Wharton research (Dec 2025) found publishers who blocked AI crawlers saw a 23.1% total traffic decline without reliably reducing citation rates, which complicates the tidy "block training, allow retrieval" advice everyone gives.

Also worth noting: AI crawlers are generally poor at executing JavaScript. A client-side-rendered site serves them a near-blank page. That check alone — does your critical content exist in server-rendered HTML — is a one-request test with a dramatic finding.

Recommendation

What to build, in order

Ordered by impact per unit of effort. The first three are the ones that change how we win a comparison; the rest close credibility gaps.

1

Multi-session sampling

Add a run index to PromptAnalytics, migrate off the (prompt, platform) unique key, run each prompt 3× per engine and store per-run results with an aggregate. Six engines × 3 gives us 18 observations to their 12 — the strongest methodology claim available in this category.

Ship a stability score alongside it: how consistently a brand appears across runs. Nobody sells that, and it directly answers the "is this win real?" question every buyer has.

Blocks our credibilityengine/core/prompt_analytics_processor.pyschema migration3× LLM cost — model it first
2

Technical SEO audit module

The largest missing surface. Build the eight monitors — robots, sitemaps, broken URLs, internal links, site vitals, images, schema, metadata — behind a scheduled crawler, rolling into a Technical Health score out of 100 with severity-ranked actions.

Cheaper than it looks: the crawling stack is already installed. backend/requirements.txt declares Crawl4AI 0.8.6, Playwright 1.58 with stealth, firecrawl 4.3.6, trafilatura, lxml and BeautifulSoup — enough to cover broken URLs, internal links, metadata, images and JSON-LD extraction without adding a dependency. Only Core Web Vitals needs something new, and that is the free PageSpeed Insights API.

Sequence it: robots and vitals first (highest value, least code), then broken URLs and metadata, then internal links, schema and images.

Widest gapnew backend appnew frontend sectioncrawler already vendored+ PSI API key
3

Free AI crawler check, public and ungated

One page, no login: enter a domain, we fetch robots.txt, classify every AI user-agent as training / retrieval / user-fetch, and tell them exactly which citations they are losing. Add the server-rendered-HTML check in the same request.

This is a day or two of work — it is two HTTP requests and a user-agent table, and the parsing libraries are already vendored. It produces an alarming and true finding for most sites, and it is the lead magnet Pixis puts behind a demo booking. Ungated beats theirs.

2 dayslead generationungated beats their demo wall
4

Publish safety and impact loop

Diff review before publish, version history, one-click rollback, and a before/after panel that ties ranking and citation movement to a specific publish event. We already publish to a CMS; we just do it without a safety net or a feedback loop, which is exactly the half they use to beat everyone else.

backend/contentties Reports to publish events
5

Diagnosis, not just measurement

Classify every tracked prompt into the four buckets — present, absent-with-competitors, open lane, engine-specific gap — and attach the matching fix. We hold all the signals already; this is aggregation and copy, not new data collection. It turns our dashboard into advice, which is the exact criticism Pixis levels at every competitor.

mostly aggregationhigh perceived value
6

Publish the denominators

Show numerator and denominator next to every visibility figure in the UI, expose run count and engine coverage, and adopt the IAB four-layer language (presence / prominence / portrayal / persuasion) with the ≥50-prompt threshold. Cheap, and it makes methodology a selling point rather than a thing we get asked about in a demo.
